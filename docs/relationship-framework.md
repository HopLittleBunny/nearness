# Nearness relationship framework

Status: research-informed, experimental product framework; not a validated clinical or psychometric instrument.

## Three questions that stay separate

1. **What is this relationship?** Bond system, specific role, social worlds, forms and life chapters.
2. **How does it feel and function?** User narration plus evidence-bound observations about companionship, mutual knowing, responsiveness, reliability, support fit, practical help, emotional security, mutuality, repair, boundaries and belonging.
3. **Am I caring for it as I intend?** The user’s selected intention, cadence and capacity compared with incomplete visible history.

Message volume answers none of these by itself.

## Evidence model

Nearness keeps four evidence states visible:

| State | Meaning | Authority |
|---|---|---|
| Observed | Pattern in imported history | Descriptive, never definitive |
| Told | The user’s lived account | Outranks model inference |
| Desired | The user’s current intention | Drives Care |
| Unknown | Missing calls, meetings, history or another person’s experience | Remains unknown |

Every AI observation must name a research construct, cite message IDs included in that run, state confidence, name missing evidence and permit correction. The model cannot silently convert an inference into a fact.

## Multi-axis ontology

One relationship can occupy several axes at once.

- **Bond system:** household/intimate, kin family, chosen family, friendship, role/community, acquaintance/contextual.
- **Specific relationship:** user-authored role such as sibling, old friend, teammate, mentor or former colleague.
- **Social worlds:** childhood, school, university, professional, sports, parenting, neighbourhood, faith/community, migration/diaspora, online, creative, volunteering, health/caregiving, personal, family or custom.
- **Forms:** dyadic, group-carried, mixed, activity-led, disclosure-led, practical-support-led, long-distance, seasonal ritual, role-bound, mentor, caregiving, bridge, household/interdependent or unclear.
- **Felt closeness:** essential, inner, active, wider world, historical/dormant or intentionally distant—placed by the user.
- **Trajectory:** new, deepening, steady, cyclical, quiet-but-intact, changing form, drifting, strained, repairing, dormant-meaningful, resting, concluded, ambiguous or unknown.
- **Intention:** understand, preserve, deepen, revive, repair, reframe, boundary, rest or conclude.

Family appears in the same atlas but uses a different interpretive grammar. Kinship, obligation, household interdependence and caregiving are not assessed as friendship cadence.

## Local communication signals

Nearness can calculate dates, coverage period, active months, conversation episodes, gaps, initiation share, attachment share and visible open-loop language. These remain communication descriptors. They are not a relationship score and do not move a person in the atlas.

## Care alignment

Care alignment is intentionally narrow:

```text
user-selected intention
+ optional user-selected rhythm
+ last visible meaningful trace
+ coverage and missing channels
+ current capacity
= aligned / mostly aligned / under-invested / needs context / intentionally quiet
```

Weekly suggestions:

- exclude people without an intention;
- exclude rest, boundary and conclude intentions;
- put visible promises/open loops before generic check-ins;
- adapt action type to relationship form and user communication preferences;
- show at most three suggestions;
- leave at least 30% of weekly time unallocated;
- support complete, lower effort, dismiss and intentionally quiet states without streaks.

Cadence prompts such as weekly, monthly or quarterly are interface choices, not scientific prescriptions.

## Prohibited inferences

Nearness does not infer mental-health diagnosis, attachment style, narcissism, toxicity, abuse, romantic/sexual intent, whether another person cares, deception, personality type, cultural style from name/nationality/language, emotional ability from gender, quality from sentiment, closeness from volume, safety of reconnecting or mutual consent to analysis/sharing.

Decline, strain, imbalance, emotional safety, growing intimacy, family burden, exclusion, support deficit and unrepaired rupture require especially cautious wording and user confirmation.

## Research lineage

The framework synthesizes, without copying a single scale:

- Antonucci’s Social Convoy Model for user-centred, life-course relationship structure.
- Aron, Aron and Smollan’s Inclusion of Other in the Self work for subjective closeness distinct from contact volume (DOI `10.1037/0022-3514.63.4.596`).
- Lee’s empirical friendship types across life stages (DOI `10.1016/j.socnet.2022.06.001`).
- Hall’s friendship formation and ideal-expectation research (DOIs `10.1177/0265407518761225`, `10.1177/0265407512448274`).
- Oswald, Clark and Kelly on positivity, supportiveness, openness and interaction in friendship maintenance (DOI `10.1521/jscp.23.3.413.35460`).
- The interpersonal process model of intimacy and perceived responsiveness.
- McGill Friendship Questionnaire functions as a broad construct source, not a universal scoring instrument (DOI `10.1037/h0087080`).
- Ambivalent-tie, chosen-family, group/dyadic, weak-tie and kin-versus-friendship research.
- Wrzus et al.’s life-course social-network meta-analysis (DOI `10.1037/a0028601`).
- Hall’s gender-expectation meta-analysis and Hyde’s gender-similarities hypothesis, used to reject individual prediction from gender (DOIs `10.1177/0265407510386192`, `10.1037/0003-066X.60.6.581`).

Before Nearness claims a measure is validated, it requires expert construct review, cognitive interviews, psychometric pilot work, measurement-invariance/bias testing and longitudinal validation against users’ own judgments—not message volume.
